# Changelog

All notable changes to NILS v1 are recorded here. The format follows [Keep a Changelog](https://keepachangelog.com/en/1.1.0/) and versions follow [Semantic Versioning](https://semver.org/spec/v2.0.0.html). The first release will be 1.0.0; until then pre-releases are tagged `v1.0.0-alpha.N`. The history of v0, the 0.x line, is in [kineuro/nils-legacy](https://github.com/kineuro/nils-legacy).

## [Unreleased]

### Added

- A source place is a dataset (HTTP API contract version 5). It declares what arrives in it, `identified`, `deidentified` or `coded`; its two trees, `derivatives/dcm-original`, the originals the pseudonymiser alone reads, and `derivatives/dcm-anon`, the pseudonymised tree the registry reads, or the folder itself where there is no such layout, which is how every place declared before keeps working; the identity rule its files are read under, as `nils digest --identity-rule` reads a file, which a digest of the dataset takes when it names none of its own; what an identifier the linkage store does not know does, `hold` or `code`; the cohort every digest of it feeds; its tag lists, `keep_demographics`, `remove` and `keep`; and what becomes of the originals, `kept`, `vaulted` or `purged`. `POST /api/places` and `PUT /api/places/{id}` take these beside the place's own fields, under `data:work` beside `places:work`, which the capabilities and the policy rows say; `nils place add` and `nils place set` take them as `--arrives`, `--identity FILE`, `--unmapped`, `--cohort` or `--no-cohort`, `--keep-demographics` or `--no-keep-demographics`, `--remove TAG`, `--keep TAG`, `--originals` and `--move-into-anon`, and `nils place list` shows what arrives and the trees. Declaring a source place looks at its folder: a v0 cohort folder's `derivatives/dcm-raw` is renamed `derivatives/dcm-anon` and nothing else is rewritten; an identified folder's loose entries are moved into the originals and an empty pseudonymised tree is made beside them; a de-identified or coded folder's are moved into the pseudonymised tree when asked with `move_into_anon`. Every move is a rename inside the folder, top-level entries only, and the answer carries `layout` with a v0 folder's counts. A folder that is a tree of another dataset is refused. `POST /api/ingest/look` answers `layout` for a folder before it is declared. `GET /api/sources` and `GET /api/places` show the dataset, each tree with its path and the files, bytes and last write the last probe counted, refreshed by `?probe=1` and never by a walk on every call, and what the pseudonymiser holds for want of a map, zero until there is one; each recent digest carries `pseudonymised`, null until a pseudonymise step precedes it. `@name` now resolves to the dataset's pseudonymised tree wherever a location is named, in a queued command, the picker's doors, `nils ingest probe` and `nils digest`, and `@name/originals` to its originals, which a digest is refused since the registry never points at an identified file; `nils place bindings` shows both. The registry's schema is at version 42 after this record's four migrations; the first, 39, adds `place.dataset` and the `pseudonym_file` table the pseudonymiser records every file of the originals in.
- The pseudonymiser (record 26). `nils pseudonymize @dataset` rewrites an identified dataset's originals into its pseudonymised tree: the code in `PatientID`, resolved through the dataset's identity rule and the linkage store exactly as the digest resolves it; the patient, provider, trial and institution groups removed tag for tag; `PatientAge` written; sex, weight and size kept unless the dataset opts out; the dataset's `remove` and `keep` lists applied, keep winning; every date and UID kept; the private elements dropped except what the pack's release list names; the overlays and curves dropped; the pixel bytes copied from the source untouched. The copies land under `<code>/<StudyDate>-<8 hex of the study UID>/<series>/<instance>.dcm`, never under the path they came in on, written `.part` and renamed, hashed on the way, the tree synced once per run. A run resumes by size and modification time (`pseudonym_file`, one row per file, with its directory), writes a changed source again over its own place, reads only the held rows a map released or a person coded anyway with `--held`, rehearses with `--dry-run`, and stops at its next heartbeat on a cancel with what was written kept. A file the linkage store does not know is held with its shape, keyed lookup and sealed identifier when the dataset says `hold`, and one review item `identity.unmapped` per dataset and shape, counts and the shape only, is opened by the runs and superseded by the run that holds nothing under it; when it says `code`, or a person asked for the file to be coded anyway, the subject is made and marked `provisional`, with one `identity.provisional` item per subject. The run is a batch of kind `pseudonymize`, its report (`files`, `subjects`, `tags_removed`, `private_removed`, `bytes`, `seconds`) the batch's counts and the job's result, and the job's progress every heartbeat. The verb takes a dataset by its name alone and refuses one read in place. A digest of an identified dataset's tree reads the codes verbatim under the `subject-code` type, which every linkage store now holds, so a subject the pseudonymiser made is found by its code. `POST /api/jobs` queues `pseudonymize` under `data:work` at detail sensitive. Migration 40 adds `ingest_batch.kind`, `subject.provisional` and `pseudonym_file.dir`.
- The chain (record 26 §7). `POST /api/jobs` takes `then: [command, ...]`, the command lines a worker queues one after another when the job before ends done, under the principal, grants and detail recorded on the first job; a step the recorded grants do not reach ends the chain and the job's result says which step and why (`chain_stopped`). A job answers `then` and `chain {before, after}` on `GET /api/jobs/{id}`, in `GET /api/jobs`, in the event stream and in `nils jobs show --json`. `bring-in @dataset [--name N] [--pack P]`, at the door and as `nils bring-in`, queues the thread of a dataset: pseudonymise, then digest, fingerprint and classify for an identified dataset, or the digest and the rest for any other, the digest sharing the pseudonymise step's name. A job queued at a door records the caller's grants.
- The batch is the thread (record 26 §14). `GET /api/batches/{id}` answers `stages {pseudonymised, walked, digested, classified, reviewed}`, read off the batch and the one it shares a name with on the other side of the thread, the jobs over its stacks and the questions on them; `GET /api/batches` and `nils status` say each batch's `kind`; `GET /api/timeline/batch/{id}` exists; `GET /api/sources` fills each recent digest's `pseudonymised` from the step before it, lists no pseudonymise step as a digest, and carries `rates {pseudonymize, digest}`, the files per second of the last run of each on this machine.
- A release plan names tags to keep and to remove beside its categories, which is how the pseudonymiser keeps the demographics and applies a dataset's lists; the release itself names none.
- The reader frames a file for rewriting, `nils_dicom::read_framed`: the header as before, and where the pixel data begins and ends, found by walking the bytes.
- Cohorts have doors, and a dataset feeds one (record 26, sections 8 to 11). A digest of a dataset whose `cohort` names one opens a membership interval for every subject it creates, and for one it meets whose membership is not open, with `source = digest` and the batch; the cohort is made on first use, owned by the job's principal and described as fed by the dataset, audited as `cohort.create` and `cohort.join` with the counts, and the digest's report and the batch's record carry `joined {cohort, subjects, met, created}`, or the refusal when the name could not be a cohort's. `GET /api/cohorts` lists every cohort with its subjects, sessions and stacks, the datasets feeding it, where it came from (a source, a promotion, a hand or an import), the open review items about its members, the releases naming it and whether it is retired; `GET /api/cohorts/{name}` adds the joins and removals grouped by batch, handle or hand act, the sources holding the members and the releases; `POST /api/cohorts` makes one and refuses a name a cohort or a selection holds; `PUT /api/cohorts/{name}` renames, keeping the id and what recorded the old name, re-owns, describes, retires or brings back; `POST /api/cohorts/{name}/members` adds and removes by code with a reason, and refuses a code the registry does not hold before anything is written, naming the codes. Reading is `data:see`, writing `data:work`, and every act is audited and moves the epoch. `nils clinical cohort list|show|make|rename|set|retire|add|remove` do the same. A retired cohort keeps its members and its history and leaves `GET /api/summary` and the ask's catalog. `nils ask promote` and `POST /api/ask/handles/{id}/promote` take a complete handle at session or stack grain too, filing the distinct subjects of its rows with the grain and the row count on the intervals. `GET /api/review?cohort=` keeps the items whose subject holds an open membership, `GET /api/review/summary?cohort=` counts the open items by kind and by cohort with the items about no member under `none`, and `GET /api/quarantine?cohort=` narrows to the batches that fed the cohort. `GET /api/explain/{stack}` answers what `nils explain` reads, from one reading both share: each axis with its value, the pack's label, confidence and tier, the evidence under it and the decision that set it, with the why. Migration 41 adds `cohort.retired_at`, `cohort_member.batch_id` and `release.policies`.
- A release reads each dataset's leaving policy (record 26, section 13). When neither `--dates` nor `--uids` is given, the policy of each file comes from the dataset holding it, the `handling.on_release` of the source place whose pseudonymised tree holds the file's source root, and a file under no dataset leaves under the run's defaults; a release spanning datasets applies each dataset's to its own files, refuses the shift-with-preserved-UIDs combination under the dataset's name, keeps one date offset per subject and one UID remapping for the run, and lets the strictest policy in play rule the tree's one clinical export. The flags override every dataset's for the run, and the release row says so in `policy.from`; the row records `policies: [{dataset, dates, uids, from}]` beside `policy`, the report, `GET /api/releases` and `nils release` carry them. `--dataset NAME` and `datasets` on `POST /api/releases` select the files under a dataset's tree, and the door takes `uids`.
- The identifier map in any shape (record 26). `nils linkage import` learns the roles of a CSV's columns with `--column HEADER=identifier:<type>`, `--column HEADER=canonical:<type>` (the identifier that stands for the person, from which the subject's code derives under the registry's scheme and key, exactly as a digest derives it), `--column HEADER=code` and `--column HEADER=ignore`; the old `--id-type`, `--id-column` and `--code-column` still work and mean the same, and v0's `PatientID,subject_code` file imports as it is. Several identifier columns in one row are several identifiers of one subject; an empty cell is skipped; a row resolves to one subject by its code, by its canonical identifier, or by an identifier already known, and adds the rest. The map is validated whole and then applied, and reports what it will do before writing: subjects named, known and new; identifiers filed, known and new; held files released; subjects merged; and the conflicts, on which nothing is written. `--dry-run` prints the report and writes nothing, `--make-types` makes the types the columns name, `--json` prints the report, and the report is the job's result. The identifiers never appear in the report or the audit. `POST /api/linkage/imports` takes the same map as JSON, answers a dry run at once and queues a `linkage import` job from a file it writes under the registry home (mode 600, removed when the job ends) otherwise; more than 100,000 rows in one call are refused with 413. A map that names an identifier a held file carries releases the file for the next pseudonymise.
- A later link merges. `nils linkage merge <canonical> <alias> --why TEXT`, `POST /api/linkage/merge` (a `linkage merge` job) and `nils linkage link`, which records the link as before and then merges, re-point every row of the alias that carries a subject to the canonical one in one transaction (studies, series, fingerprints, memberships, clinical rows, picks, handover subjects, handle and list members, decisions at subject scope; the session cache of both is dropped for the next rebuild), close a membership of the alias that duplicates an open one of the canonical with `left_by = merge`, move the alias's identities, file the alias's code on the canonical subject as an identifier of the new type `subject-code`, leave the alias row marked `merged_into` and `merged_at` (migration 42, which also seeds the `subject-code` type) and out of every listing and count, close its provisional review item, and record `subject.merge` in the audit with both codes, which moves the epoch. An import whose row maps an identifier of one existing subject to the canonical identifier of another merges the first into the second, and says so first.
- Identifier types have doors: `GET /api/linkage/types` lists each type with how many identifiers and subjects it holds, `POST /api/linkage/types {name, description}` makes one, once by name; `nils linkage types` prints the same. The held files of a dataset have doors against the pseudonymiser's table: `GET /api/linkage/held?place=` answers the shapes with counts, `POST /api/linkage/held/code {place}` marks them to be coded anyway on the next run, and `POST /api/linkage/held/reveal {place}` answers the identifiers themselves by shape, one `read_audit` row per identifier. Reading identifiers (the map applied, the reveal, the merge) needs `data:work` at detail sensitive; the dry run's report and the held list hold no identifier and need `data:work` and `data:see`.
- Every axis value's word list is a site's to amend through an overlay (pack contract 5). An overlay carries `lists` beside its `buckets`, each list named `axis.value` by the value's identity or its label, with the words it adds and removes; the merge is the buckets' (the pack's words, then the additions deduplicated case-insensitively with the first spelling kept, then the removals), applied when the pack loads and never written into it. A value the axis tries by a flag alone gains a word tier from a list. A value no word of the pack's reaches (a route sets it, or it is the default), a value or an axis the pack does not have, and anything but words (a flag, a physics window, a threshold, the order, or the keys of a value) are refused, and the refusal says what stays the pack's. The overlay document has a schema of its own, `contracts/pack/v5/overlay.schema.json`. An engine at contract 5 loads a contract-4 pack unchanged; the MRI pack stays at contract 4. `POST /api/classify/try` and `POST /api/overlays` take `lists`; `nils overlay show` prints them and `nils overlay export` writes them; `nils classify --overlay` applies them; adoption re-sorts the scope as before; `overlay_unused` counts a list's terms as it counts a bucket's.
- `GET /api/packs/{name}` and `nils pack show --json` answer every axis in the order it is decided with its values in the order they are tried: each value's name, label and family, whether the axis tries it, its keywords after any overlay, the bucket they came from, the list an overlay amends it by, the detection clauses in words (the exclusive flag, the alternative flags, the flag combinations, the physics windows as written with their confidence and why), and the terms the registry's adopted overlays put on it. Beside the axes: the flags count, the review thresholds, every amendable list, the site's terms per list and the adopted overlays. `GET /api/packs` says how many `lists` and `buckets` each pack opens. `nils pack show` prints the count of lists.
- `GET /api/classify/signals` answers `by_value`: per axis and value, how many stacks were decided to it and by which kind of clause (`by_flag`, `by_word`, `by_physics`), how many a person is still asked about (`unsure`), the words shadowed on the way to it and the words the rule cited on stacks a person then decided otherwise (`overrode_with`); and `origins`, the manufacturers, models and stations of the scope with their counts. Bounded as the signals are.

### Changed

- The digest's identity resolution is one `Resolver` the writer and the pseudonymiser share; a code read verbatim is no longer a display-code collision with the subject whose code it is, whatever identifier that code was derived from.
- A promotion is a cohort act, so `POST /api/ask/handles/{id}/promote`, the queued `ask promote` and its cancel need `data:work` where they needed `release:work`, and a promotion into a retired cohort or under a selection's name is refused.
- `nils explain --json` prints the document the explain door answers: the evidence sits under each axis with the decision that set it, where it was one list beside the axes; the text is as before.
- A source place declared with nothing said about it arrives de-identified, where its handling read as identified; `handling.arrives` mirrors the dataset's `arrives` and takes `coded` too.
- `nils setup` names every graphics card the machine has and the memory of them all, as in `2 × NVIDIA RTX PRO 6000, 191 GB`, where it named only the first card nvidia-smi listed. The supervisor's install report carries `machine.cards`, each card with its name and memory, beside `machine.card`, which is the card with the most memory and what the advice reads.

### Fixed

- A merge keeps a cohort membership whole, so `nils linkage merge`, `POST /api/linkage/merge` and an import whose row merges two subjects no longer end on a unique constraint over `cohort_member` when alias and canonical joined one cohort in one digest, which is the common case. An identifier map applies as one transaction on each store, so a map that is refused halfway leaves no subject behind it.
- An identifier map releases a held file whatever type it names the value under, and its report says how many files each identifier released. A map that named a held value under another type released nothing and said nothing about it.
- A job that fails under the worker records what the verb printed, not its exit status alone, so a card on Pipelines says why it stopped.
- A pseudonymise dry run queued at the door records its report as the job's result, where the report existed only on the command line's own output.
- `POST /api/ask/run` with `keep` and a name a kept handle holds already is refused in words, where it ended on a unique constraint.
- A job keeps the command line it was queued with under `args.queued`, `argv` says what ran, and the queue's worker is no job the doors list.
- The pseudonymiser leaves an original the pseudonymised tree holds already as it is, so a v0 cohort folder's files are not written a second time beside v0's own, and `bring-in` on such a dataset digests the tree it has before it pseudonymises anything.
- A pseudonymised tree's codes are read verbatim whatever their length, so a v0 cohort folder's sixteen hex characters are read as the codes they are: with v0's map filed, a person keeps the code they had and is one subject and not two. A value no subject holds stands as its own code where it has the shape this registry makes, and is otherwise an identifier a code is derived from.
- A release whose dates move numbers its sessions in date order instead of refusing to run, since the dates are each dataset's own and the labels are the tree's; the release row records the scheme that named them and the report says why it is not the scheme that was asked for. A months or ordinal scheme named by the caller stands as it is, and dates that move with UIDs preserved is still refused.
- A digest of a dataset read in place reads what it says of an identifier the linkage store does not know. Where the dataset says `hold`, the file is not filed: its `source_file` row is quarantined under `identity.unmapped` with the shape of the identifier, no subject is made for it, and one review item per dataset and shape says how many, opened and closed as the pseudonymiser's are; a held file is read again by the next digest, so a map filed since releases it. Where it says `code`, the subject is made and marked provisional, as the pseudonymiser marks it.
- `POST /api/ingest/look` takes `path`, the absolute path of a folder under no ingest location, which is how the desk looks at a folder before a dataset is declared on it; the look is the same one and bounded the same way, and the answer names no location.
- A dataset's subjects are the subjects whose files its tree holds, whoever made them, on `GET /api/sources` and on a batch's digested stage. They were the subjects a digest created, so a dataset whose subjects an identifier map made read as none.
- `GET /api/sources` takes each rate from the last run of that step that read or wrote at least a hundred files, else the largest run there was, and says how many files it was measured over: `rates` carries `{files_per_s, files}` per step. A resume run of ten files reported eighty thousand files a second.
- Each recent digest on `GET /api/sources` names the jobs of its thread by stage under `chain`, where it carried none.
- A cohort counts the sessions of its members out of the session cache as it stands, and answers none where nobody has built it; every cohort answered zero. `GET /api/summary` and `GET /api/sources` say the window the cache was built under.

## [1.0.0-alpha.28] - 2026-09-15

### Changed

- The engine checks grants and detail in place of the role ladder (suite contract version 2, HTTP API contract version 4, MCP contract version 2). A caller holds a set of grants, each naming a page and how far it goes there (`see`, or `work`, which includes see; the assistant has `use`), and a detail, `plain`, `quasi` or `sensitive`, for how much of a record it sees. A token's `grants` and `detail` claims are taken as they are. `--role GROUP=X` and `--token TOKEN=user@node:LIST` take grants beside the ladder names, which stand for their sets: reader is `query:work`, `data:see` and plain; reviewer adds `review:work`, `pipelines:see` and quasi; operator adds `data:work`, `release:work`, `pipelines:work`, `places:work`, `install:see`, `kvasir:see`, `assistant-settings:see` and sensitive; admin is every grant but `assistant:use`; `assist` is `assistant:use`. A caller left with no grant is refused with 403. Every door needs its grant, which its policy row and a refusal name; `POST /api/jobs` checks the grant of each verb, a cancel the grant of the job's verb, and adopting an overlay needs `review:work` and `data:work`. Saving a selection, uploading a list and starting from one need detail quasi, the viewer's pixels quasi and burned-in annotation sensitive, and projecting raw identifiers sensitive. `X-Nils-Ceiling` keeps the grants of its step's set and `assistant:use` and lowers detail to the step's. The capabilities carry `grants`, `detail` and, for one release, `roles` as the ladder steps up to the detail; the MCP door's metadata names the grants as its scopes. A subject that already holds `@` is taken as the principal only from a trust entry that keeps subjects, `--oidc-trust ...,keep_subject=true`, as the desk's own entry is to; any other entry qualifies every subject by the issuer's host, as before.
- Setup, where people sign in through a provider, has the engine and Kvasir trust the tokens the desk signs beside the provider's, marks the desk's trust entry as keeping its subjects, and names the provider's groups claim in the desk's configuration; `nils update --all` mends Kvasir's trust on an install from before. The install takes Kvasir 1.0.0-alpha.7 and the assistant 1.0.0-alpha.24, which read grants.

### Fixed

- `NILS_TOKENS` keeps an entry's own list: `T1=bo@lab:reader,kvasir:see` is one entry, as `--token` takes it, since a piece that holds no `=` belongs to the entry before it. The variable was split at every comma, so an entry listing more than one name stopped `nils serve` at start.
- A job queued through `POST /api/jobs` records the caller's detail, and a job that recorded none runs as plain; an `ask run` queued there ran with every class. Of the ask verbs that door queues `ask run` and `ask promote`, and no other.
- `GET /api/review`, `GET /api/review/{id}`, `POST /api/ask/values` and `GET /api/instances/*` need what their handlers needed, the stricter of the two, now that each policy row is read from the door table.

## [1.0.0-alpha.25] - 2026-09-14

### Added

- `nils setup` installs llama.cpp's server wherever it installs the assistant, so a model Kvasir downloads can be started from Kvasir without a model server of your own. It takes llama.cpp build b10964 for this machine: on Linux the Vulkan build where there is a graphics card or a render node and the CPU build otherwise, on macOS the build for its processor. The archive is checked against the sha256 this version pins and unpacked into `<dir>/llama.cpp/`, and setup names the devices llama.cpp runs a model on. The plan says when a Linux machine has no Vulkan loader, since a model then runs on the processor until one is installed. llama.cpp runs as `nils-llama.service` on this machine whichever runtime the parts use (a launchd agent on macOS), started before Kvasir, in router mode with no model loaded and at most one at a time. It listens only where Kvasir reaches it (127.0.0.1:7110 by default, docker's bridge for a docker install), with a key only Kvasir and llama.cpp read, and keeps its presets, key and log in `<dir>/kvasir/runtime/`. `kvasir.json` names it under `local.runtime`, and where Kvasir runs in a container, `hostAlias` names how Kvasir reaches this machine's own loopback. An archive that cannot be downloaded, or whose sha256 is not the pinned one, stops an install; `NILS_SETUP_LLAMA_RELEASES` names a mirror, held to the same digests. `nils update --all` takes the build a new version pins, or takes it for an install from before that has the assistant, and restarts it. `nils uninstall` removes it.

### Fixed

- `nils uninstall` stops the services it removes from this machine on a docker install too. It removed the supervisor's unit there and left the supervisor running.

## [1.0.0-alpha.24] - 2026-09-14

Released beside nils-desk 1.0.0-alpha.24 so the two stay in step. The engine itself is unchanged since 1.0.0-alpha.23.

## [1.0.0-alpha.23] - 2026-09-14

Released beside nils-desk 1.0.0-alpha.23 so the two stay in step, and with Kvasir 1.0.0-alpha.4 and nils-assistant 1.0.0-alpha.23, which `nils setup` now takes at those tags.

### Added

- `POST /api/ingest/folders` and `POST /api/ingest/look` let an operator choose what to digest from the engine's own ingest locations, where no supervisor answers. The first lists the locations, each with the place that holds it, or a page of the folders inside a folder named as `@root/relative`, filtered by name and paged after a name, at most 1,000 a page, each folder with whether it can be opened and the place that holds it. A folder's names are kept for a minute while it does not change, so a folder of a hundred thousand folders is read once for all its pages. The second says what a few folders hold: a sample of sixteen files each, read breadth first and sniffed for DICOM with their modalities and scanners, within a budget the folders share, and a folder the budget did not reach says so, to be asked for again. Nothing outside the ingest locations is listed: a parent step, a leading slash and a link that leaves its location are refused, links inside are left out as the digest leaves them out, and a listing reads the disk for at most five seconds.
- `nils setup` offers your ChatGPT subscription as what the assistant talks to, where nobody signs in. Once Kvasir runs, setup shows a link and a code, waits while you sign in with ChatGPT, and sends the assistant's purposes that read no rows of the archive to the subscription; the prompt then leaves your systems. The purposes that read rows stay closed to it until an admin opens them in the desk's settings, or stay on a local model that serves them. A sign-in that fails or outlives its code stops an install, and running `nils setup` again and keeping the subscription signs in. With nobody at the terminal no sign-in is started. Where people sign in, a subscription is each person's own, signed in from the desk, and setup offers none.

### Changed

- `nils setup` sets up Kvasir, the model gateway, the way Kvasir now holds its models itself. `kvasir.json` is written with where Kvasir listens, how it knows its callers and the purposes the assistant uses, and names no model. The model you choose is added through Kvasir once it runs, and taken only once it answers from where Kvasir runs: a model Kvasir cannot reach stops an install and is said on an update. Kvasir warms and admits a local model it is given on its own, and setup waits for the admission. Until Kvasir holds the model, it is kept with its key beside `kvasir.json`, readable by nobody else, so a machine install with no services, or a Kvasir that did not come up, has it added by the next repair. The screens say Kvasir where they said the gateway.
- An update or a repair of an install from before takes the backends out of its `kvasir.json`, which Kvasir no longer starts with, and adds each back through Kvasir under the same id, with the key its key file holds. The `model.key` an earlier setup wrote is removed.

Eight steps, each answerable by a flag and each skippable: **what to install** (the engine alone, the engine and the desk, or everything, which adds the assistant and the model gateway); **where it runs** (on the machine as two binaries, or in podman or docker containers, detected in that order); **where it lives** (one directory holding `registry/`, `desk/`, `backups/`, `working/`, `export/`, and a directory of DICOM the engine may read, which becomes an ingest root and a `source` place); **the registry** (a key passphrase asked for twice and never echoed, through the terminal's own echo flag rather than a new dependency, and SQLite or Postgres, the connection tried before anything is written so that a wrong connection string costs a sentence rather than half a registry); **who may sign in** (nobody, the desk keeps the people, or an identity provider, one answer setting the engine's `--auth` and the desk's `mode` together, and then who may open the desk, where choosing the network while nobody signs in says plainly that anyone on that network who finds the port gets the whole registry and offers the login instead); **what this machine can do**; **keeping it running**; and **the plan**, which is a summary before a single file is written. - What the graphics card can serve, said before the assistant is offered and in the product's own words. `nvidia-smi`, then `rocm-smi`, then an Apple machine's unified memory: 24 GB or more fits a 27B model at 4 bit with room for the context, which is what the stations were written against; 12 to 24 GB fits a 7B to 14B model and the stations still work with more retries on the harder questions; under 12 GB or no card at all there is no local model worth serving, and the options are a model on another machine you can reach, a commercial provider through the gateway (the prompt then leaves the machine, and the gateway marks that backend remote), or no assistant at all, which costs nothing else. The wizard never installs a model.
- Containers as a first-class choice, not a port of the machine path. A podman run is one pod named `nils` publishing only the desk's port, the engine and the desk inside it, and `:U` on every bind mount so that a rootless container owns what it reads; a docker run is a network named `nils`, the desk publishing the port, and no `:U`, because docker does not remap the user. The images are `ghcr.io/kineuro/nils` and `ghcr.io/kineuro/nils-desk`; where an image cannot be pulled a Containerfile is written beside the base directory and the image is built there from the release binary already downloaded, and the wizard says so. The registry is made by the engine either way, running the key and the init steps inside a container against the mounted volume. Podman is kept running by quadlets in `~/.config/containers/systemd/`, docker by a `compose.yaml` in the base directory, the machine by systemd user units on Linux and launchd agents on macOS; where there is no service manager the commands are printed instead. Windows is refused with a sentence and a link, not a stack trace.
- The places a fresh install declares, in the order the registry's own rule needs: the backup place before the registry that names it, then the source directory where one was given, the working directory and the export directory. An engine that serves no places door says nothing and installs the rest.
- `~/.config/nils/setup.toml` (or `$XDG_CONFIG_HOME`), which is the wizard's memory: the base directory, the parts with their versions and how each one runs, the mode, the runtime, the service manager, the ports, the reachability, the backend and the places declared. Run `nils setup` again and it opens with what is installed, where, in what mode and how it runs, then offers to update everything, change something, add a part, or **repair**, which writes the configuration and the units again from what the state records, asking nothing.
- `nils update --all`: every part the state names, each in the way that part runs. A binary is replaced from its release as before, a container is a pull of the new tag and a restart of its unit with the unit rewritten to name it, a Node part is a fetch and a rebuild. One line each, and the engine last, since it replaces the binary doing the replacing. The desk's releases are its own repository unless `--channel` or `NILS_DESK_RELEASES` says otherwise.
- Prompting that survives `curl ... | sh`. Where stdin is a pipe the wizard opens `/dev/tty`, and where there is no terminal at all it takes every default and says so rather than waiting for an answer that will never come. `--yes` is the same thing on purpose, `NILS_NO_TTY=1` says it to a script that does have a terminal, and `--print` says what would happen, with the exact commands and unit files for both container engines, and changes nothing. Where there is no terminal to ask a passphrase on and no `--key-file`, one is made from the machine's own randomness and written to `<dir>/key.passphrase` readable by nobody else, with a line saying to move it into a password manager, rather than leaving a registry nobody can open.
- The assistant's two Node parts, installed the only way they can be: Node 22 checked, the repositories cloned and built, the gateway's `kvasir.json` written from its own example with the port this setup chose, an admin token made here and the address given for the model (a backend that is not on this machine is marked remote, and the wizard says that the prompt then leaves the machine), `assistant.env` written with everything the assistant reads, and the assistant's key minted at the gateway once the gateway answers, the command printed where it is not up yet. Where node is missing the wizard says so and installs the rest. It never installs a model.
- [`docs/guides/setup.md`](docs/guides/setup.md), the guide, and an installing section in the README.

## [1.0.0-alpha.1] - 2026-09-10

### Added

- `nils update`: the newest release over this binary, checked against the release's `SHA256SUMS`, written beside the old one and renamed over it; `--check` says what it would do, `--version` takes one, `--to` installs elsewhere, and `--channel` (or `NILS_RELEASES`) points the whole thing at a deployment's own release directory instead of the project's. The packs in use are refreshed with it when they are the user's to replace, and a release now carries `packs.tar.gz` beside the six binaries and their checksums.
- A pack directory a fresh install can find. Without `--pack-dir` or `NILS_PACK_DIR` the engine looks in the registry's own `packs`, then `$XDG_DATA_HOME/nils/packs` (else `~/.local/share/nils/packs`), `/usr/local/share/nils/packs`, `/usr/share/nils/packs`, and `share/nils/packs` beside the binary's prefix, taking the first that holds a pack; `nils serve` names the directory it took and `nils pack list` says where it read from.
- The repository: licenses, contributor terms (CLA and DCO), security and trademark policies, templates and CI, as the design record decided (`docs/decisions/`, R1 to R8).
- The engine skeleton (Wave 1 spec, §14, slice 1): the `engine/` Cargo workspace with `nils-dicom`, `nils-registry`, `nils-digest` and the `nils` binary, on Rust 1.98.0 pinned; CI formats, lints and tests on SQLite and Postgres 16 and builds `nils` on the six release targets; a tag now attaches the six binaries and their checksums to the release. The synthetic corpus generator moved from the spike to `tools/synth/`.
- The reader and the walker (Wave 1 spec, §14, slice 2). `nils-dicom` reads Part 10 files with or without the preamble and bare implicit or explicit VR data sets, stops before Pixel Data, carries the field catalogue (v0's 171 columns with their sources, fallbacks, converters and sensitivity classes, rendered to `docs/reference/catalogue.md`), the Enhanced functional-group and private per-frame fallbacks, the six DWI private values by creator block, the character set handling, the seven quarantine classes and the diagnostics. `nils-digest` walks a root with a pool of listing threads (symbolic links skipped, an unlistable directory a `walk_error`, an unlistable root the job's failure), filters names with the `files` knob and feeds a pool of parsers. `nils digest <root> --dry-run` reads everything and prints the report (counts per quarantine class and diagnostic kind, studies, series, subjects, modalities, SOP classes, transfer syntaxes, character sets, rate, peak RSS); `--describe` prints the knobs; `--json` for both. Over the nmosd corpus (508,045 files) it refuses the 134 files the spike refused, at 62,300 files/s on eight workers.
- The schema and the writer (Wave 1 spec, §14, slice 3). `nils-registry` declares the registry and the linkage store once as data (fifteen and six tables, the catalogue's columns typed from the reader's declaration) and renders them for SQLite and Postgres through a small dialect layer with numbered migrations, a key store (`nils key`), and the bulk paths: multi-row inserts on SQLite, `COPY` into temporary tables merged with `INSERT ... SELECT ... ON CONFLICT` on Postgres (`NILS_PG_BULK=insert` selects multi-row inserts there too). A registry is a home directory with `nils.toml` (`--registry`, `NILS_REGISTRY`, `NILS_DSN`); `nils init` creates one on either backend. `nils digest` now writes: one transaction per batch, subjects under the pseudonym scheme (the identity rule is fixed until slice 4), studies, series and their modality tables, instances, every path with its status, the diagnostics and the epoch; a known row is never rewritten, a stored null is filled by the first file that has a value, and a file that disagrees raises `field_disagreement`. Resume by default: unchanged files are skipped by `(size, mtime_ns)`, quarantined ones kept unless `--retry-quarantine`, changed ones re-read and filed with a `file_changed` diagnostic, and a complete unfiltered walk marks the paths it did not see `gone`. Jobs: a job row with a heartbeat, refusal while another job is fresh (exit code 3), takeover of a stale one, and `nils status`. The synthetic generator became the `corpus` example of `nils-dicom`, writing the files with the crate's own Part 10 writer. The synthetic million (1,011,783 files, 2,000 of them refused by design) digests to identical counts on SQLite, Postgres `COPY` and Postgres `INSERT`: 32 s, 65 s and 70 s on 64 workers from cache, at 2 GB peak RSS with the batch queue capped at sixteen (it was 4 GB uncapped); the numbers are in the spec, §9.2.
- Identity (Wave 1 spec, §14, slice 4). The two pseudonym schemes (`blake2b-8`, v0's function byte for byte, and `blake2b-32` with its display code), the subkeys of the linkage store derived from the registry's one key, the identity rule as a YAML file (`nils digest --identity-rule`: fields in order, a pattern with an `id` group, the study UID as the fallback), and the writer's resolution through the linkage store: a returning identifier is one subject across studies and runs, an imported code is kept, a subject holds one identifier per type, and two identifiers on one code roll the batch back, open an `identity.collision` review item and fail the job without naming an identifier. `nils linkage import` files a CSV of legacy codes validate-then-apply; `linkage id-type add | list`, `linkage show --why` (decrypts, writes `read_audit`), `linkage link` and `linkage unlink` exist as the spec lists them. A linkage store carries its registry's id and is refused next to another registry. The report gained `subjects_matched` and `identities_attached`. `serde_yaml` gave way to `serde-saphyr`.
- Stacks (Wave 1 spec, §14, slice 5). Every instance is filed under a stack of its series, decided from the file alone by v0's signature: fourteen values in v0's order and rounding (the MR timing, echo and coil fields, the CT exposure fields, the PET bed and frame type, the orientation class from ImageOrientationPatient by v0's cross product, and ImageType), keyed by BLAKE2b-8 of a canonical string. The `stack` row carries the key, the index in order of first appearance across batches and runs, the class, the confidence and the fourteen values as read; the instance points at it from the transaction that files it. A known orientation under 0.9 counts `orientation_oblique` once per stack created; the thirteen series-level columns a signature is also made of are left out of `field_disagreement`, because instances that differ on them are the series' stacks. The report and the dry run count `stacks`, and `written` counts `stacks_created`. On the nmosd corpus v1's partition equals v0's, recomputed with v0's own code (`spikes/stacks/`), on every one of the 2,165 series both hold: 2,534 stacks, 2,534 v0 groups, over 493,708 instances.
- Jobs, resume and custody (Wave 1 spec, §14, slice 6). One signal stops a digest (what is parsed is written and committed, the batch is `cancelled`, exit code 130) and a second abandons the batch in flight; a job whose process is gone is taken over at once; a failed batch records `reparse_from` so that the run that resumes it reads its last second again and repairs a registry commit that lost its linkage commit. The kill-and-resume tests (`NILS_DEBUG_STOP`, a test hook) show a run killed after a commit, killed inside a transaction, stopped, aborted or interrupted resuming to the same rows as an uninterrupted one, on both backends. `nils custody [--json | --markdown]` lists every store a registry keeps (the configuration, the registry, the linkage store, the key store, the quarantine list, the job records, the logs) with where it lives, what it holds, how long, and the commands that read, change, export and delete it; the test checks that every file under the home is listed and every command named exists, and `docs/reference/custody.md` is the page for a SQLite home. `nils quarantine list` prints the refused files with class and detail, one `ingest.quarantine` review item per batch and class is filed with the count as evidence, `nils review list | show` read the items, and `nils linkage purge --subject <code> | --all` deletes a subject's identifiers and linkages after saying what it would delete, keeping the read audit and recording itself as a job. Unchanged files no longer pass through the parsers' queue (the resume stage batches them for the writer itself): a second pass over an unchanged half-million-file tree takes 4.7 s where it took 25.8 s, on any number of workers.
- The compare tool (Wave 1 spec, §14, slice 7). `tools/v0-compare/` (Python over DuckDB) reads a v0 registry through `export.sh`'s read-only CSV export or a DSN and a v1 registry on SQLite or Postgres, and measures the gate of §12: every v0 instance in v1 or explained by name, every v1 instance in v0 or explained (v0's file mode, its nine SOP classes, its modalities, its resume rule, a series v0 never wrote), every field agreeing after both sides are brought to one normal form (v0's list literals to the backslash form, canonical numbers, ISO dates and times, JSON as presence, v0's rounding on the six rounded stack columns), the stack partitions matched by membership, every v0 subject code a v1 code with every study on the same code, and v1's sessions against v0's events. Divergences are grouped by field and pattern as shapes, never values, and an adjudication file classes each group `v0-bug`, `v1-bug` or `accepted`; a group in the first or the last class is excused from the bar it would fail, the others count in full, and the report (`report.md`, `report.json`, counts and shapes only) ends with the bars and an exit code. `linkage-csv` writes the CSV `nils linkage import` takes, counting collisions; `--key-file` classes how v0 derived each code. The walker's `files` knob takes a comma-separated union (`--files dcm,no-ext` is v0's "all"), the corpus generator gained `--same-day-percent`, and a `v0-compare` CI job projects a v0-shaped export out of a synthetic registry with injected divergences and checks that each lands in its class and a clean one passes, on both backends.
- What the first gate run taught the compare tool (Wave 1 spec, §12.1 to §12.3, slice 7). A SQLite registry is read by its declared types rather than as text, since SQLite spells a REAL with 15 significant digits and a float32 widened to a double needs 17, which made hundreds of series differ on the spelling alone. The thirteen stack-signature columns of the series tables are derived from the catalogue as the engine derives them, and a divergence of one of them in a series with several stacks on either side is grouped apart (` (multi-stack)`) and classed `accepted` by the tool itself: the series row carries the first instance's value on both sides and which instance is first follows the walk order; the same field in a single-stack series still needs a rule. A v0 path absent from the compared root is reported apart when its subject is listed under several cohorts, since a v0 path is relative to its own cohort's root; `--fs-cap` bounds the on-disk check (a million paths by default, `0` for all of them) where it used to stop silently at a hundred thousand. `export.sh` counts lines, not rows.
- A row's fields are decided, not raced (Wave 1 spec, §8 and §9.1, slice 3 amended in slice 7). Where two files of one subject, study or series disagree on a column, the row keeps the smaller value in the catalogue's canonical text order instead of the first one written, on every column of those rows; the fill rule covers every column too. The registry no longer depends on the order the walk and the workers gave the files, which the first gate run showed to be real: single series carry up to twenty-six spellings of `sequence_name`, two acquisition matrices, two slice spacings and two orientations. `min` needs no bookkeeping, so a resumed run, a late batch and a second digest of one tree reach the same row; the writer reads a field's stored value once, the first time that field of that row is disagreed about, and keeps it with the cached row.
- A person may hold several identifiers of one type (Wave 1 spec, §7.3 and §7.4, slice 4 amended in slice 7). `nils linkage import` files any number of identifiers of one type on one subject instead of refusing the second, and counts the ones that joined a code another identifier already named: a personnummer is not for life, since a temporary number becomes a permanent one when residency is granted, several temporary ones may come before that, and the permanent one changes when a legal sex change does. Only an identifier that maps to two codes is still refused, and two identifiers that derive one code by the scheme's function are still a collision. The identity rule gained `code: verbatim`, which files the value it reads as the subject code instead of deriving one, for data pseudonymized before it reaches us; it requires a pattern on every field, so a value that is not shaped like a code is never filed as one, and a file that does not match takes the fallback as always.
- Files whose elements declare a length their VR cannot hold are read, not quarantined (Wave 1 spec, §6.1, slice 2 amended in slice 7). One vendor's private block writes a `UL` of six bytes, where a `UL` takes four; `dicom-rs` reads the values that fit but counts the whole length as consumed, so the stream runs two bytes behind and the file looks truncated. The reader now repairs such a file in memory when the first read failed as truncated: it walks the header, rounds each impossible length down to the values that fit, drops the surplus bytes and reads the repaired copy, counting a `ragged_length` diagnostic. The file on disk is never touched. On the mix corpus this recovered 1,996 files of 196,086, and with them 69 series, 47 studies and 32 subjects.
- The budget, measured (Wave 1 spec, §14, slice 8; §12.5 and §12.6). On an 8 vCPU / 64 GB virtual machine reading the corpus from the storage server over NFS, from a cold cache, v0 digests one study's raw tree of 497,150 files in 981 s at 507 files/s and 5.78 GiB, and v1 in 87 s at 5,713 files/s and 0.83 GiB, with the same counts on both sides: eleven times the rate at a seventh of the memory. On Postgres 16 v1 takes 93 s; a second pass over the unchanged tree takes 4.2 s. The gate of §12 passes on that host too, every bar. A `bench` job in CI writes 200,000 instances from a seed, digests them and fails the build when the rate falls below 80 percent of the recorded baseline or the memory rises above its cap (`engine/benches/`).
- The fingerprint (Wave 2 spec, §4 and §13, slice 1). A new crate, `nils-classify`, and a new table, `stack_fingerprint`, added by migration 2 so that a registry written before it opens under this binary without being rebuilt. `nils fingerprint` derives one row per stack: the six text fields v0 joins, each folded and once more as the join (Unicode NFKC, whitespace collapsed, **case kept**, because a pack's first normalizer step is a case-sensitive removal); the multi-valued fields as read, since a parser declares its own tokenizer; the physics, the stack's own value where it has one and the series' otherwise; the shape, the provenance and the contrast administration; and v0's field of view, from the stack's first instance **by SOP Instance UID** rather than by row id, which is Wave 1 §15's open item closed on the deterministic side. It is a job like the digest, with the same takeover of a dead one, a heartbeat, a stop between windows and a resume that is a predicate over columns: a stack already derived whose instance count still agrees is skipped, one that gained an instance is derived again, and `--force` overwrites. What the fingerprint does **not** hold is the decision the slice turned on: no flags and no semantic normalization, because both are derived by a pack's grammar. The fingerprint holds what is true of the file; a pack holds what is true of the knowledge, and correcting the token map is then a pack version rather than a re-extraction of the archive. It does carry one thing v0 computes and never reads: **why a series split**, the first signature column its stacks disagree on in v0's order, which three of v0's flags test for and none of them ever receives. v1's derivation agrees with v0 on all 2,165 series of the nmosd corpus and on 2,559 of the mix corpus's 2,567; the eight are v0's own, and their cause is named: v0 writes the key in its sort step and not in extraction, so all 19 multi-stack series in the archive without one are exactly the 19 that were never classified. On the development container the pass costs 0.8 s for nmosd's 2,534 stacks and 0.4 s for the mix corpus's 4,120, at 0.08 GiB, against the 30 s and 87 s their digests took.
- The pack loader (Wave 2 spec, §5 and §13, slice 2). A new crate, `nils-pack`, which knows nothing about a registry: a pack plus a stack yields facts, which is what makes a pack testable from a fixture and shippable by someone who has never seen our schema. It loads a manifest (name, semantic version, contract, modality), the parser and flag layers, and the pack's editable **buckets**; it refuses a contract it does not implement, a version that is not semantic, a name that resolves to nothing, a pattern that will not compile, a flag that depends on itself, and a parser declared twice, each **by file, line and path**, because a pack is written by someone who writes vocabulary rather than Rust. **Overlays** (C2) are provenance-scoped and refused when scoped by a selection; the merge is v0's rule exactly, duplicates dropped case-insensitively with the first spelling kept and the order of writing preserved, since v0's contrast vocabulary contains terms whose leading space is load-bearing. The pack's own **corpus runs at load** and its failure is the load's failure, which is what makes a pack from elsewhere safe to install. Whose cases judge what was settled while building: the pack's corpus judges the pack, un-overlaid, and an overlay carries its own cases and is judged by those, because running the author's claims against a site's amendment makes the claims a constraint on the site. `packs/mri/` holds the parser and flag layers the C11 prototype verified against v0 over 518,365 stacks, now with 18 cases of its own; `nils pack list | show | validate` read them.
