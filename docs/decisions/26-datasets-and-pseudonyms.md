## What was found

**In the engine (alpha.28).** The data path is separate queued jobs a person starts one by one (digest, fingerprint, classify, pick, session, pyramid, release, handover); nothing chains and nothing runs by itself. For display it already answers everything a page needs: `GET /api/sources` (digests with counts), `/api/batches[/{id}]`, `/api/quarantine`, `/api/jobs` with cancel, `/api/events` (the open jobs every second), `/api/timeline/{kind}/{id}`, `/api/depends`. Identity is resolved at digest: PatientName and PatientID are stored nowhere in the registry; the identifier is sealed in the linkage store beside it, keyed by the registry's one key, every read audited; many identifiers of many types on one subject is the design (a personnummer is not for life); `nils linkage import` files identifier → code pairs; `nils linkage link` records a canonical subject and an alias, and nothing reads the link. A source place is never written. De-identification is the release: a written policy on the release row (patient, provider, trial and institution tags removed, PatientID replaced by the code, PatientAge computed, dates kept, shifted or cut to the year with the offset in the linkage store, UIDs remapped from the key, shift with kept UIDs refused, private tags dropped except the pack's allowlist, overlays and curves dropped, a file that says nothing about text in pixels held). `place.handling` is declared and displayed and read by nothing. The only verbs that write DICOM are release and handover. Packs are data: the mri pack has eleven axes, 138 flags and 106 word lists; a site may amend four of them (the `buckets`) through an overlay scoped to a manufacturer, a model, a station or a batch, rehearsed with `POST /api/classify/try`, proposed with a review item beside it, adopted by a person holding `review:work` and `data:work`, which re-sorts the scope and invalidates dependent handles. `nils explain <stack>` says why one scan was judged so, on the command line only. A cohort is a membership fact: subject grain, an interval log with provenance, opened by a promotion of a complete subject-grain answer or by a clinical import; no door makes, renames or retires one, and none adds or removes a member by hand.

**In the desk.** The Data page reads one door every twenty seconds and shows source cards with four marks; there is no job surface, no batch page, no refused files, no way to start the sorting. Review, Release and Pipelines are placeholders. Six finished modules have no caller: the stage strip, the job composer, review triage, release state, the release body, and a complete image viewer no page mounts. The knob-engine client functions have no caller; `keyword-tune` and `identity-check` cannot be picked in the chat.

**In the assistant.** Five stations. `keyword-tune` surveys the classifier's signals, writes a prediction before rehearsing, rehearses, checks the diff and proposes an overlay; `identity-check` probes two identity rules over a sample, shapes only, and proposes one; `operator` turns an instruction into a plan whose reversible steps a scheduler fires under a standing grant. No station may adopt, decide, release or change a rule.

**In v0** (its 0.5 checkout, and the group's registry). A cohort was three things joined by a lowercased name: the folder, the pipeline card in the application database, and the subject membership in the metadata database. The anonymiser chose the code (five PatientID strategies), wrote it into PatientID, deleted PatientName, and kept the map beside the images and in an audit table (how that map was protected is not part of this copy, as in 16). Extract hashed whatever PatientID it found (seed = the cohort name, upper-cased there and lower-cased in the writer's fallback) unless a CSV mapped it; an identifier missing from the CSV was hashed with an INFO log. Extract had no path source; a regex miss in the folder strategy passed the original identifier through. Nima kept one person one subject by building the CSV by hand: every personal number a person ever had, and every study identifier, mapped to the one code derived from the canonical number. The group's registry today: eight identifier types the group named itself (registry and study ids), 5,322 subjects, 4,205 of them carrying more than one type, 1,803 in more than one cohort. v0's tag list: four groups (patient 34, trial 23, provider 38, institution 5) that v1 carries tag for tag, plus nine date tags v1 made the date policy; v0 dropped no private tag, overlay or curve and never rewrote a UID; its UI listed 17 codes its own table lacked, five never removed and one mislabelled.

**By measurement** (studies/2026-09-15-pseudonymize-spike, on the laptop, 19,200 synthetic files of 133 KB). Plain copy 2,800 files/s, bounded by write-back at 370 MB/s; a move is a rename, 6 ms. Digest 16,000 to 24,000 files/s, headers only, saturating at four workers on the single writer. The release's rewrite, the closest proxy for the new step: 1,330 files/s, 176 MB/s, one thread, CPU-bound, the whole file parsed into memory, written, read back to hash, no fsync. A lean writer that rewrites the header and streams the pixel bytes: 4,250 files/s on one thread, 14,000 to 17,000 files/s on 8 to 16 threads, 1.3 GB/s to disk; an fsync per file collapses it to 279 files/s. `dcm-anon` keeps the originals' SOP UIDs, so the two trees must never be digested into one registry; the verbatim rule on `dcm-anon` alone reproduced every code.

## Nima's decisions

- Shape C, the batch is the thread: the four pages stay, and every batch gets its own page with the stage strip; "I like Shape C · The batch is the thread on Data page and the design overall. We can refine it later for small details."
- A dataset is one folder with one name and may feed a cohort. A cohort is membership and nothing else: make, rename, retire, add, remove; a query's answer becomes a cohort and its card appears at once.
- **The registry never points at an identified file.** Identified data is pseudonymised first, into its own tree, and only that tree is read. "We just need to have more sophisticated anonymizer to create dcm-anon and only digest from that." Pseudonymisation is "an optional but real step like digest and release", DICOM tags only; no pixel change (defacing is a pipeline).
- The trees are `derivatives/dcm-original` and `derivatives/dcm-anon`; a `dcm-raw` found is renamed. De-identified data is moved into `dcm-anon`, and the map asked for "unless does not care and want to hash it to random subject code".
- The golden rule: "a unique subject remains the same subject no matter where it comes or how many types under how many tries", which is why UIDs are kept in `dcm-anon`: "if we get new ingest and we have same study and same stack how would we know if we do not keep UIDs?"
- An unmapped identifier holds its files, and "user should know we hold them until the map is provided."
- The originals stay by default; vault or purge as options: "we rarely purge the original data, we just handle it differently."
- A later link merges: "of course."
- The map in any shape: "as flexible as possible." Identifier types are arbitrary, chosen from the registry's list or made on the fly; a subject carries many.
- The page is named for what it is: Pseudonymisation.
- Rules: every axis's word lists are the site's to grow, not four; the assistant may help later.

## Decisions taken on that

1. **A dataset is a place with the source role**, named, with two trees: the originals (`derivatives/dcm-original`, read by the pseudonymiser only, never a source) and the pseudonymised tree (`derivatives/dcm-anon`, written by the pseudonymiser only, the registry's only source). A dataset declared on a folder with no such layout reads the folder itself as its pseudonymised tree, which is how every place declared before this record keeps working. A folder holding `derivatives/dcm-raw` is a v0 cohort folder: `dcm-raw` is renamed `dcm-anon` on declaration and nothing is rewritten. "A source is never written" becomes "a source is written by the pseudonymiser only".
2. **What arrives** is declared per dataset: `identified` (pseudonymised into the tree before anything reads it), `deidentified` (moved into the tree as sent, in place; its identifiers mapped at read time), or `coded` (our codes already in PatientID, taken verbatim). Moving is a rename on the same filesystem; nothing is copied.
3. **Pseudonymise** is a verb and a job kind. For every file of the originals it reads the header, resolves who the file is about through the dataset's identity rule and the linkage store exactly as the digest does, writes a copy with PatientID = the code, PatientName and the patient, provider, trial and institution groups removed (v0's list, tag for tag), PatientAge written, sex, weight and size kept unless the dataset opts out, dates and times and UIDs kept, private tags dropped except what the pack names by creator, overlays and curves dropped, the dataset's own remove and keep lists applied, the pixel bytes streamed untouched, into a layout from facts (code, study, series, instance) and never from the original path. It resumes by size and modification time, writes `.part` then renames, hashes while writing, syncs once per batch, caches directories, and runs per-file workers under bounded channels like the digest. It records every file (written, unchanged, held, refused) and the tags it removed by count. Its identity reads run at detail sensitive.
4. **An unmapped identifier holds its files.** When the dataset says `hold` (the default for identified arrivals and whenever a map is declared), a file whose identifier the linkage store does not know is not written; it is recorded with its shape (digits as 9, letters as A) and a keyed lookup, counted on the batch and on the dataset, and raised as one review item per dataset and shape. When the dataset says `code`, the file gets a code derived from the identifier under the key and its subject is marked provisional. A map that names a held identifier releases its files; the next bring-in writes them.
5. **The map is any shape.** A CSV's columns are named for what they are: an identifier of a type, the canonical identifier that stands for the person (the code derives from it), the code itself, or ignored; several identifier columns in one row are several identifiers of one subject; a type is one of the registry's or made with a description. It is validated then applied, and reports before writing what it will do: subjects named, known and new; identifiers filed, known and new; held files released; subjects merged; conflicts (an identifier already on another subject), on which nothing is written. v0's file (PatientID, subject_code) imports as it is. Filed once, it holds for every dataset.
6. **A later link merges.** Linking an alias to a canonical subject re-points every row of the alias (studies, stacks, memberships, identities, decisions) to the canonical one in one transaction, files the alias's code as an identifier of type `subject-code` on the canonical subject, leaves the alias row marked merged and excluded from every door, and records `subject.merge` in the audit and moves the epoch. An import that maps an identifier of one existing subject to the canonical number of another merges the same way, and says so first.
7. **Bring in what is new** is the chain: pseudonymise, then digest the pseudonymised tree, then fingerprint and classify with the dataset's pack. `POST /api/jobs` gains `then`: commands queued when the job before them ends `done`, under the principal, grants and detail of the person who queued the first; a step the person may not queue stops the chain and the batch says why. A dataset that arrives de-identified or coded has no first step.
8. **A dataset may feed a cohort**: every subject a digest of it creates joins that cohort, the interval recording `source = digest` and the batch. The rule can change; taking it away keeps every member.
9. **Cohorts have doors**: list with counts and provenance, make, rename, retire, add and remove members by hand with a reason (`source = manual`, `left_at` written), promote from a card at session or stack grain by taking the subjects of the rows. The cohort acts are Data work. A retired cohort keeps its members and its history and leaves the lists.
10. **Review takes a cohort**: the queue, its summary and the quarantine filter by the subjects' open memberships; a subject in two cohorts shows in both and is decided once.
11. **Explain has a door**: `GET /api/explain/{stack}` answers what the command line prints.
12. **Every word list is the site's to grow.** An overlay names `lists` by `axis.value` beside the old `buckets`; the pack's flags, physics and the order values are tried in stay the pack's. The signals report by value. Pack contract 5 says so; an engine at 5 still loads a contract-4 pack.
13. **Release reads the dataset's leaving policy** (`on_release`: dates, UIDs) for the files of each dataset, and a release spanning datasets applies each dataset's to its own files; the release row records them.
14. **The batch is the thread.** `GET /api/batches/{id}` reports the five stages (pseudonymised, walked, digested, classified, reviewed) with their counts and jobs; the timeline kind `batch` exists; the sources door reports the trees, the held counts and each batch's chain.
15. **Detail on the wire stays record 25's.** Reading identifiers (the map, the held reveal, the merge) needs `data:work` and detail sensitive; the held list itself, shapes and counts, is `data:see`.

## The contract between the parts

OpenAPI version 5. Every door below carries `data:see` unless said otherwise; the shapes are what the desk builds against.

- `GET /api/sources`: each source gains `arrives` (`identified|deidentified|coded`), `trees {originals: {path, files, bytes} | null, anon: {path, files, last_written}}`, `identity` (the rule), `unmapped` (`hold|code`), `cohort` (name or null), `tags {keep_demographics, remove[], keep[]}`, `held {files, identifiers}`, `originals_kept` (`kept|vaulted|purged`); each recent digest gains `pseudonymised {files, changed, held, job}` and `chain [job ids by stage]`.
- `POST /api/places` and `PUT /api/places/{id}` (`places:work` and `data:work` for the dataset fields) accept `arrives`, `identity`, `unmapped`, `cohort`, `tags`, `move_into_anon: true` (de-identified arrivals), and answer `layout {v0: {original_files, raw_files, renamed}}` when they found one. `POST /api/ingest/look` answers `layout` for a folder before it is declared.
- `POST /api/jobs` accepts `then: [command, ...]`; verbs `pseudonymize @dataset [--name N] [--held]` (`data:work`, detail sensitive) and `bring-in @dataset` (the chain). `GET /api/jobs/{id}` shows `then` and `chain` (the job before and after).
- `GET /api/batches/{id}` gains `stages {pseudonymised: {files, changed, held, refused, job}, walked: {files, new, changed, unchanged, refused, job}, digested: {stacks, sessions, subjects, moved, job}, classified: {stacks, of, unsure, pack, jobs}, reviewed: {done, of, since}}`; `GET /api/timeline/batch/{id}` exists.
- `GET /api/linkage/types` and `POST /api/linkage/types {name, description}` (`data:work`).
- `POST /api/linkage/imports` (`data:work`, sensitive): `{place?, columns: [{header, role: identifier|canonical|code|ignore, id_type?}], rows: [[...]], dry_run}`; a dry run answers the report `{subjects: {named, known, new}, identifiers: {filed, known, new, types_new}, held_released, merges: [{alias, canonical}], conflicts: [{row, why}]}`; otherwise 202 with a `linkage import` job whose result is the same report.
- `GET /api/linkage/held?place=`: `[{shape, files, first_seen, batch}]`; `POST /api/linkage/held/code {place}` (`data:work`): code them anyway, 202; `POST /api/linkage/held/reveal {place}` (sensitive, audited): the identifiers themselves.
- `POST /api/linkage/merge {canonical, alias, why}` (`data:work`, sensitive): 202, a `linkage merge` job.
- `GET /api/cohorts`: `[{name, owner, description, subjects, sessions, stacks, feeds: [place names], from: {source|promotion|manual|import, detail}, waiting, releases, created_at, last_joined, retired_at}]`; `GET /api/cohorts/{name}`: the above plus `joins: [{when, what, subjects, by, batch|handle|actor}]`, `sources_holding`, `releases[]`; `POST /api/cohorts {name, owner?, description?}`, `PUT /api/cohorts/{name} {name?, owner?, description?, retired?}`, `POST /api/cohorts/{name}/members {add: [codes], remove: [codes], why}` (all `data:work`); `POST /api/ask/handles/{id}/promote` takes any grain and `{cohort, create, reason}` and files the subjects of the rows, moving from `release:work` to `data:work`.
- `GET /api/review?cohort=`, `GET /api/review/summary?cohort=` (`{by_kind: {kind: count}, cohorts: [{name, open}], none: count}`), `GET /api/quarantine?cohort=`.
- `GET /api/explain/{stack}` (`review:see`): `{stack, pack, version, overlay, axes: [{axis, value, label, confidence, tier, evidence: [{rule_set, rule, source, matched}] | decision: {kind, actor, why}}]}`.
- Rules: `GET /api/packs/{name}` answers every axis with its values, labels, keywords, flags and thresholds; `POST /api/classify/try` and overlays take `lists: {"axis.value": {add[], remove[]}}`; `GET /api/classify/signals` gains `by_value`.
- `POST /api/releases` takes `datasets` as a selection too; the release row records each dataset's leaving policy.
- Job kinds: `pseudonymize`, `linkage-import`, `linkage-merge` join the queue; every job records the caller's detail as before.

The suite's grant vectors are unchanged. The desk pins OpenAPI 5 and refuses an engine below it as it does today.

## Acting on the originals, and what it cost

Nima asked, after the wave was otherwise proved, why a dataset's originals could be declared kept, vaulted or purged while nothing acted on it. Nothing did, because no slice had been given it: it is the one destructive act in the record, and it had been left as a recorded intention. It was then built on both sides, and five labs is what it took to make it safe.

The vault was right the first time and survived every attack: a rename where one filesystem allows it, otherwise a copy whose digest is compared before the source is removed, a stop that leaves no file in neither place, symlinks not followed, and no way to move identified files into a tree the registry reads.

The purge destroyed data twice before it was right, and both times only a lab looking for it noticed while every test passed. First: verification asked whether the copy was what had been recorded, and never whether the original was still the file that had been copied, so an original replaced after its copy was written was deleted and the older copy survived. Then, with that closed at rest, two more ways: a file changed while the walk ran was judged on the size and time captured when its directory was listed, many files earlier, and an original whose timestamp was put back to the recorded value was invisible to any check made of two numbers.

What settles it: **a purge proves by content what it destroys.** The pseudonymiser records the original's own digest as it copies (it already reads every byte), and the purge reads each original again at the moment it removes it, measuring before and after the hash so a write landing mid-read is caught, and leaving anything that fails with its reason named. A row written before that digest existed is refused as unproved rather than trusted. The survey stays cheap and says outright that its counts are a forecast.

Decisions this produced, beyond the record's own: an act, never a declaration, writes what became of the originals, so `originals_kept` and `originals_vault` are refused at the places doors; a purge is refused while any file waits for a map, because a held file's original is the only copy of that person's scans; and a stopped job says stopped, which fixed every cancellable verb, not only these.

The labs are in studies/: 2026-09-16-lab26 (the wave, 25 findings), lab26b (all re-tested, ready to release), lab26c (the purge loses data), lab26d (it still does, twice), lab26e (it no longer does, six files). The last was six files because the faults are per-file properties; the earlier ones used thousands, which is why they were slow, and the walk race is now proved by a test-only seam on two files in a fifth of a second.
